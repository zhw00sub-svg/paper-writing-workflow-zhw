# English Academic Prose

Use this reference for ZHW's English medical and scientific manuscripts, including drafting, translation, and polishing. It implements the same preference for complete, natural reasoning as the Chinese policy, while allowing English to develop in its own syntax and register. It is part of the existing bilingual writing skill, not a separate translation workflow.

## Begin With Meaning

Identify what the passage establishes, how the evidence supports it, and what remains uncertain. Preserve the population, comparator, time interval, endpoint, estimates, and inferential strength. A linguistic revision must not add a mechanism, a clinical recommendation, an unreported analysis, or an implication of causality.

If a Chinese draft is supplied, reconstruct the passage from its meaning rather than translating its sentences in order. Sentence boundaries and local ordering may differ. Return to the source to check that no fact, condition, or qualification has been lost.

## Build An Argument Readers Can Follow

Each paragraph should do the work its section requires. Methods describe what was done; Results report what was observed; Discussion interprets the evidence; a review explains how studies address related or unresolved questions. Do not require every paragraph to contain a finding, a mechanism, an implication, and a limitation.

Let sentences connect through their subject matter. Introduce the information needed to understand the next claim, and identify the actual comparison or unresolved question. Use however, therefore, and furthermore only when that relationship exists. Neither a transition word nor a concluding phrase can repair a missing premise.

Develop the argument with necessary explanation, not repeated paraphrase. After “Regular follow-up was associated with less treatment discontinuation,” an additional sentence saying “These findings suggest an association between follow-up and persistence” adds nothing. State a necessary limitation once, where it controls interpretation; do not repeat it in successive forms.

## Write In Idiomatic Scientific English

- Use identifiable subjects and informative verbs. Replace vague phrases such as “the implementation of the evaluation of clinical value” with the actual action or observation.
- Retain established terminology. Weight loss maintenance, treatment discontinuation, and risk prediction can be appropriate technical nouns; nominalization is a problem when it hides the meaning, not merely because it is a noun.
- Use active or passive voice according to emphasis. Do not rewrite every Methods sentence into “we,” or remove the investigator when that obscures who performed a procedure.
- Keep the conditions that make a result intelligible. A well-constructed complex sentence may be clearer than several clipped statements. Do not impose a 15–20-word average, one-clause rule, or artificial sentence-length variation.
- Name the actual result instead of appending “highlights the importance,” “provides a framework,” or “has significant implications.” Keep such wording only when the passage explains precisely what it means.
- Preserve stable terms instead of rotating synonyms for elegance. Repetition of a necessary technical term is preferable to an ambiguous pronoun or a misleading synonym.
- Natural prose does not require informality, rhetorical questions, idioms, invented anecdotes, or exaggerated certainty. No vocabulary blacklist or AI-detection score establishes writing quality.

## Match Interpretation To Evidence

Use language appropriate to the actual design and analysis. An observational association does not establish the effect of an intervention; randomization alone does not justify every secondary or mechanistic claim. Be explicit about the relevant limitation rather than stacking may, might, potentially, and possibly.

Distinguish what the study measured from what a future study could test. A clinical application belongs in the text when the research question supports it; do not manufacture an application to make a descriptive finding sound important.

## A Worked Example

The following facts are fictional and provided only for a writing exercise: a single-center observational study included 120 adults, followed them for 12 months, found a lower proportion of discontinuation among regular follow-up attendees, did not randomize participants, and did not measure costs.

**Unhelpful wording:**

“Optimization of follow-up implementation dimensions supported the enhancement of treatment persistence. These findings provide a framework for extending the value of longitudinal management pathways.”

**Revision:**

“In this single-center observational study of 120 adults, patients who attended follow-up regularly were less likely to discontinue treatment during 12 months of observation. Because follow-up attendance was not randomly assigned, this association does not establish that regular follow-up improved treatment persistence. Costs were not measured, so the study cannot assess the economic implications of these follow-up arrangements.”

The revision supplies the observed relationship and its relevant limits without inventing an effect size, statistical significance, mechanism, or recommendation. In an actual manuscript, include the cost limitation where economic implications are discussed; do not insert it into an unrelated Results paragraph merely to complete a template.

## Review English Independently

Read the English passage continuously, without looking at the Chinese source. Check whether the finding, reasoning, and references are understandable on their own. Then check factual equivalence against the source or claim map. These are separate tasks.

For a short edit, return the revised passage with a brief explanation of the substantive language change. For a manuscript, review all requested sections and record the actual coverage; a successful sample does not certify the whole text. If author-approved English samples later become available, use them to refine collocations and register without treating their claims as evidence for a new paper.
